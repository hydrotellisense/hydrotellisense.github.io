Observing water insights from orbit.

HydroTelliSense team combines satellite remote sensing, physics-based hydrologic modeling, and machine intelligence to monitor, predict, and provides insights on water resources and early warning of hydroclimatic hazards.

A research group focused on intelligent decision making from satellite observations.

What we work on

We are a voluntary group of Bangladeshi professionals working to advance water resources security and effective water management in Bangladesh. Located in the Bengal Delta, where three major river systems converge, Bangladesh faces complex and rapidly changing challenges related to floods, river dynamics, erosion, water availability, and transboundary water resources.

Our initiative focuses on using satellite remote sensing and Earth observation to complement ground-based data and address gaps caused by limited resources, inaccessible data, institutional fragmentation, and transboundary constraints.Satellite observations can provide consistent, objective, repeatable, geographically comprehensive, and near-real-time information on water resources and their changing conditions. We aim to demonstrate how this independent source of evidence can support researchers, water managers, policymakers, and other end users in making more informed decisions.

Our goal is simple: to turn satellite observations into practical, accessible, and actionable information for a more resilient and evidence-based water management system in Bangladesh.

Latest innovation

ROSES-2025 ECIP-ES proposal

Coupling LIS–LHASA with SMAP & NISAR soil moisture

We're replacing LHASA's precipitation-based proxy for soil saturation with physically based, observationally constrained soil moisture — assimilating SMAP and NISAR retrievals into NASA's Land Information System via an Ensemble Kalman Filter, and adding 2D lateral groundwater flow. The goal: sharper landslide prediction over the Central and Southern Appalachians, evaluated against probability of detection, false alarm rate, and ROC-AUC.

Portfolio

Landslides · Colombia

Satellite-based landslide mapping, Cauca Valley

Multi-temporal mapping using U-Net deep learning and NASA's Prithvi geospatial foundation model, benchmarked against the SALaD (Semi-Automatic Landslide Detection) OBIA/Random Forest system, with temporal compositing to handle cloud cover.
